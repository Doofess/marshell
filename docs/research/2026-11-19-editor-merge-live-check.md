## Findings

1. **The plan named the wrong key for reaching the bar.** Check 9 said "Esc, then Tab". The bar sits above the editor, as it has since 6q-4a, so the way to it is Esc and then Shift+Tab. Merge is the first button and so the last one reached from the editor. Not changed: the order "Merge, Keep mine, Take disk" is the reading order, and a person who comes from the tabs above reaches Merge first.
2. **Quick presses of F7 are announced late.** The announcer speaks at most once every 1.5 seconds (`announce-policy.ts`), which is older than this slice. Five presses 350 ms apart moved the cursor every time, and the announcer still read "Conflict 2 of 3." after the fifth; pressed two seconds apart each place was read. Not changed here: the limit is shared by everything that announces.
3. **A palette opened with the mouse takes the keyboard back to its button.** "Next conflict" run from a palette opened by clicking "Command palette" moved the cursor and announced the place, and the keyboard ended on the palette's button, not in the editor. Opened with Ctrl+Shift+K from the editor, the keyboard ended in the editor. The palette gives focus back to where it came from, as it does for every command. Not changed.
4. **On a marker line in the light scheme a selection is faint.** The marker tint (24% of a grey) and the selection (a grey of its own) are close in lightness there; the selection's edge is visible, and on the person's and the disk's lines it is clear. Not changed; for the user's eye.
5. **Not covered live.** A merge answered after the person typed again or after Keep mine (the answer is dropped or the merge runs again), the ten-second limit, and a second merge while one runs are covered by tests only (`editorDoc.test.ts`, `server/merge.rs`): none can be timed by hand against a merge that takes a few milliseconds.
