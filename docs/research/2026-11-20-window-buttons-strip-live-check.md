# Window buttons strip live check

- Date: 2026-11-20, 12:55 to 13:31, 16:55 to 17:16 and 17:31 to 17:34 local time, in four runs.
- Build: `window-strip` under `pnpm tauri dev` on Windows 11 Pro (26300), one 3440-wide display at 100%. Windows itself was in dark mode.
- Home: a scratch `MARSHELL_HOME`, `MARSHELL_CLAUDE_SETTINGS`, `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`, `CODEX_HOME`, `COPILOT_HOME` and `USERPROFILE`, with CDP on port 9333. Onboarding was finished with "Skip: use limited status" for every agent. One plain PowerShell 7 session was started for the overlay checks; no agent session was started.
- Data: none.
- How it was driven: the page through CDP (evaluations, key presses, clicks inside the webview). The strip is a native window that a CDP capture doesn't show, so it was read from PowerShell with Win32 queries (`FindWindowEx`, `GetWindowRect`, `IsWindowVisible`, `SendMessage(WM_NCHITTEST)`) and a `BitBlt` of its own device context. In the first run no mouse or keyboard input was sent to the desktop: where a check needed a button to act, the strip was sent its own action message (`WM_APP`), after its class and owning process were checked.
- The second and third runs: real input, synthesized (`SetCursorPos`, `mouse_event`, `SendInput`) with the user's permission while they were away from the PC. Every pointer action first checked that the top-level window under the point was the dev app's (for the one click in the Snap Layouts flyout, the flyout's own), every key that the dev app was in front, and nothing was sent otherwise. These runs were judged by captures of the screen (`CopyFromScreen`), not of the strip's device context: see finding 8. The second run was the branch as reviewed, the third the build with findings 8 and 9 fixed; a result below is from the third unless it says otherwise.

## What the check compares

The strip shows pictures the page paints of its own three buttons. "Match" below means a pixel-by-pixel compare of two captures of the same 138 by 40 rectangle: the page's own buttons (a CDP capture, which leaves the strip out) and either the strip's window (`BitBlt`) or the pictures `paintStrip` hands to the shell.

## Findings

1. **The plan's top band had nothing under it.** The plan had the strip pass its top rows through (`HTTRANSPARENT`) to a top-edge resize window of Tauri's. Under Tauri's `unstable` feature the app's webview is a child webview, and Tauri makes that resize window only for a window's own webview, so the band handed its points to the page's buttons instead: a click there worked, and the hover never lit. The band is gone; the strip takes the pointer over its whole height, as the page's buttons do, and the top edge resizes on the row above the client area, as on `main`.
2. **Onboarding lay over the bar with the strip floating on it.** Onboarding covers the whole window, bar included, but didn't count as covering the page. It now calls `useCoverPage`, and the scan test knows the layer (`coverScan.test.ts`, failing first).
3. **The pictures were off the page's by 114 of 5,382 pixels.** Three causes: the page paints an icon's box on whole device pixels (14.5 down becomes 15), it cuts the icon to its box (the ends of Close's strokes), and a canvas made with `willReadFrequently` draws a thin diagonal stroke differently from the page. The glyph's origin is rounded as the page's, the stroke is cut to the box, and the canvas is an ordinary one (`tiles.test.ts`). 0 differ since.
4. **At a fractional scale the layout was measured from the wrong end.** 138 CSS pixels are 172.5 device pixels at 125%. The page's buttons end at the window's right edge and each edge is rounded from there, so the pictures are now 57, 58 and 57 wide (they were 58, 57, 58). The icon's box gets the same treatment: each of its edges goes to a whole device pixel on its own (a 12.5-pixel icon becomes 12 or 13 wide) and the drawing is fitted to the box. Both are reasoned from how the page lays out, not measured: see C2.
5. **The performance panel was under the strip.** Found by the branch's review, not by this check: the panel (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+P) was fixed in the window's top-right corner and covers nothing, so the strip lay over its first lines. It now starts below the bar (`perfOverlay.css.test.ts`).
6. **The rest look was read from a button the pointer can be on.** Also from the review: with the strip hidden the page's own Minimize is under the pointer, and a read then took its hover colours for the rest look. A third hidden probe gives the rest look now, and the probes are found by their marks (`tiles.test.ts`). Not seen live.
7. **The check's own script was wrong twice.** It marked the page's buttons with the mark the strip's hover probes carry, so the looks reader took a real button for a probe (a whole-button "mismatch" on Close's hover), and Playwright's own screenshot dropped the emulated scale. Both were faults of the script, not of the app. The first showed that `readLooks` relied on the order of the marked elements, which finding 6's change removed.

8. **The strip was not on the screen.** Found by the first real hover: the page's own side-pane button lit, the strip's buttons didn't, while a `BitBlt` of the strip's device context showed Close lit red. A set of green test pictures sent to the strip never reached the screen either. wry makes the webview's window (`WRY_WEBVIEW`) without `WS_CLIPSIBLINGS`, and a window without it paints over the siblings above it. So the strip was there, took the pointer and acted on clicks, and the person saw the page's buttons under it: at rest the two are the same picture, which is why every earlier check passed. With the style set on the running app's webview window the green showed at once. The strip's code now gives every webview window of the main window the style when the strip is made and again whenever it shows (`the_webviews_window_stops_painting_over_its_siblings`, failing first). The third run confirmed it with no outside help: the style read `0x56000000`, the hover lit on screen, and a covered page showed its own buttons where the strip had been.
9. **A button stayed lit after a click moved the strip from under the pointer.** After a click on Maximize the pointer stays where it was and the strip goes to the new right edge; Restore was then drawn lit there until the pointer came to it and left again, and the same after a click on Restore (second run, on screen). The likely cause, reasoned from the code and not traced: the release ends the mouse capture, and the client area's leave message that follows finds the pointer still on the middle column and lights it, with no leave asked for from that column, which is the system's own Maximize button and has a leave of its own. A leave that is really a crossing now asks for the other area's leave, and after every move of the strip a pointer that is no longer on it lights nothing and holds no press (`a_leave_that_is_a_crossing_asks_for_the_other_areas_leave`, `a_pointer_is_idle_until_it_is_over_or_down_on_a_button`, failing first). In the third run the left-behind picture was not lit after a click on Maximize, a click on Restore, and Win+Up pressed with the pointer on Minimize.
10. **A scripted pointer closes the Snap Layouts flyout, on any window.** Not a fault of the app: a pointer that jumps from Maximize into the flyout makes Windows close it, on a plain window with a standard title bar too. A one-pixel glide keeps it open, and that is how the layout was picked.
11. **A hang the pull request's review predicted did not happen.** The review read finding 9's new check as holding the strip's lock while it asks which window is under the pointer, which asks the strip's own procedure, which takes the lock: a hang whenever the window resizes with the pointer resting on the strip. Tried in a fourth run (17:31 to 17:34) on that build: the window in the top-left quarter, the pointer on Minimize and lit, then Win+Down, which changes only the window's height and leaves the strip under the pointer. The window kept answering messages. The lock is let go before the question is asked, by a rule of the language that is easy to misread, so the code now reads the flag into a local first and no longer leans on it.

## Seen on the way, not part of this slice

- The page's own Minimize glyph is blurred over two rows at 100%, on `main` too: its stroke is centred on a pixel boundary. The strip shows the same picture.
- The bar's brand button (`aria-label="Marshell menu"`, `aria-haspopup="menu"`) has no click handler; the menu opens from the sidebar's Menu button and its shortcut.
- The launcher opened by Ctrl+Shift+T about half a second after the palette closed left focus on the palette's button, and Escape didn't close it until focus moved in. Seen twice through CDP keys, not tried by hand.
- While Doctor or Settings is open the buttons are the page's own, since the strip hides under any cover. They lag the edge on a resize there, as they do on `main`.

## Not checked

- Everything marked "Not checked" above: a display at 125% or 150%, and a click on the strip with the keyboard in a browser page.
- How the edges look to the eye while they are dragged, which is what the slice is for: the numbers say the strip is at the edge at every step, not how smooth it is.
- Picking a Snap layout and the snap keys on the third run's build: both were done in the second run, with finding 8's style set from outside and before finding 9's fix. The third run saw the flyout appear.
- Real forced colours. They were emulated; whether Windows' own contrast themes read the same through the probes is open.
- Moving the window between two displays of different scale: one display here.
